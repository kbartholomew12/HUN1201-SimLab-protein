Protein Builder: The Amino Acid Allocation Challenge

Build proteins without wasting the building blocks.

You will manage protein intake for a randomized person across seven decisions. Your choices affect protein target progress, essential amino acid coverage, protein sparing, tissue balance, and nitrogen processing. Each new case file draws from expanded food pools and avoids repeating the immediately previous set whenever another eligible choice is available.

Your goal is not to chase the highest protein number. Your goal is to match protein quantity and quality with the person’s energy needs and body demand.

Purpose

Connect protein food choices with digestion, essential amino acids, nitrogen balance, protein sparing, and tissue repair.

Task

Review the case file, calculate the assigned target, complete seven decisions, and finish one personalized Nutrition Debrief.

Criteria

Use your unique simulation data, apply at least two protein concepts, and predict how a different choice would change the outcome.

How to Use the Five Indicators

Do not try to maximize every meter. Use the indicators together to choose the food that best meets the person’s remaining needs.

Protein Target Progress

Shows how many grams have been selected compared with the assigned target.

Consider: Is the person still short of the target, near it, or already above it?

Essential Amino Acid Coverage

Shows whether the day’s foods collectively represent all nine essential amino acids.

Consider: Does the next choice fill a gap through a complete protein or complementary foods?

Protein-Sparing Support

Shows whether carbohydrate and fat are available to provide energy so amino acids can support other functions.

Consider: Is this a balanced food choice or mostly isolated protein?

Tissue Balance

Shows whether the overall pattern creates pressure toward tissue loss, maintenance, or building and repair.

Consider: Does the result match the body demand in the case file?

Nitrogen Processing Load

Shows how strongly the model is routing surplus amino acids toward deamination and urea formation.

Consider: Would more protein improve another indicator, or mainly increase processing?

A strong final pattern usually has: protein near the assigned target, all nine essential amino acids represented, moderate-to-strong protein sparing, tissue balance appropriate for the case, and a low-to-moderate nitrogen processing load.

These are learning indicators, not clinical measurements or medical advice. The essential amino acid model is simplified: foods contain amino acids in varying amounts, and the SimLab highlights whether the day’s overall pattern supplies meaningful coverage.

Decision 1 of 7 ·

How should I use the dashboard for this decision?
  1. Find the need: Check which indicator is furthest from the case goal.
  2. Compare the tradeoffs: A choice may add protein but offer limited amino acid variety or little energy for protein sparing.
  3. Avoid automatic choices: The option with the most protein is not always the best option, especially after the target and amino acid needs are met.
  4. Check the result: After selecting, use the “What Changed?” panel to see which indicators improved and which created a tradeoff.

Quick rule: Meet the protein and essential amino acid needs, support the person’s energy and tissue goal, and avoid a large unnecessary protein surplus.

Nutrition Debrief: Explain Your Protein Pivot

Write 150–250 words in your own thoughts and words.

Review your Protein Pivot of the Day. What happened during this decision, why did it cause the greatest overall response, and how would one realistic different choice have changed the outcome?

Your response must include all four parts:

  1. Identify the specific decision and choice selected as your Protein Pivot.
  2. Cite before-and-after data for at least two indicators.
  3. Explain the response using at least two protein concepts, such as essential amino acids, complete or complementary proteins, protein sparing, digestion and absorption, nitrogen balance, deamination and urea, or protein functions.
  4. Propose a different choice and predict its effect on at least two indicators.

Individual reasoning matters: refer to your assigned person, exact food choice, and unique dashboard values. Generic summaries, copied definitions, or wording that could fit any case do not meet the criteria.
